Summary

The state is funding the machines. This bill is the line for the people who run them.

Justification

Why now

On October 20, 2026 the Governor announced the New Jersey Supercomputer Initiative, a project of more than $125 million: $25 million from the State (already in the FY2026 budget), a $75 million personal gift, and $25 million from SHI International. The announcement names apprenticeships and training pathways in AI, data science, and advanced computing. That investment is for new capacity. It does not address the incumbent State workforce that already administers, secures, and develops the systems the public depends on.

Three gaps this bill closes

Gap todayWhat the bill doesSection
The live Information Technology Specialist specification requires education to get the job but no continuing education after appointment, even as the tools change yearly. Teachers, by contrast, carry a 20-hour annual professional-development duty under N.J.A.C. 6A:9C.Paid, employer-funded annual cybersecurity and role-specific instruction, with published provider standards and no exclusive provider.§6
People doing technical work under non-technical titles have no defined path to a classification review tied to the duties they actually perform.Duties-based identification standard plus a written Commission determination within a set deadline; routine software use alone does not qualify.§4–5
No requirement that an agency assess workforce effects, notify representatives, or consider retraining and reassignment before a technology-driven reduction.Workforce-impact assessment, advance notice, written response to alternatives, and documented transition planning before implementation.§7–8

What the bill deliberately does not do

  • It does not move title-setting authority away from the Civil Service Commission; the Commission already holds the State classification plan under N.J.S.A. 11A:3-1, and the bill works through that framework.
  • It does not guarantee a title, salary range, or continued employment on identification alone (§5d, §8b).
  • It does not make Local 787 or UTECA an automatic training provider; approval runs through published standards open to colleges, labor-management programs, nonprofits, and others (§6e–f).
  • It does not touch call-center volume rules or school-district partnerships; those remain separate policy concepts.

Full draft

Bill text

Synopsis

Requires identification and appropriate classification of State technology work; establishes annual technical and cybersecurity training for covered employees; requires workforce-impact reporting and transition planning before technology-driven reductions; and appropriates $1 million for initial implementation.

An Act

Concerning the State technology workforce, establishing a continuing education and workforce transition program, supplementing Title 11A of the New Jersey Statutes, and making an appropriation.

Be It Enacted by the Senate and General Assembly of the State of New Jersey:

1. Short title

This act shall be known and may be cited as the “New Jersey Public Technology Workforce Development and Transition Act.”

2. Legislative findings and purpose

The Legislature finds and declares that:

  1. The effective operation, security, and modernization of State technology systems depend upon a skilled public workforce.
  2. Changes in artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, software development, data management, networking, and related technologies require continuing development of employee knowledge and skills.
  3. Employees who perform substantial technical duties should have access to a process for determining whether their assigned duties are consistent with their position classifications.
  4. Continuing education should support employee development and public service, rather than create an unfunded obligation or an automatic basis for displacement.
  5. Agencies proposing workforce reductions attributable to technology should assess the effects on employees and consider training, reassignment, and redeployment before implementing those reductions.
  6. The purpose of this act is to establish coordinated duties to identify technology work, provide continuing education, maintain appropriate classifications, and plan for workforce transitions.

3. Definitions

As used in this act:

“Commission” means the Civil Service Commission.

“Covered agency” means a department, office, board, commission, or other agency in the Executive Branch of State government whose covered positions are subject to the State classification plan.

“Covered employee” means an employee of a covered agency who: (1) holds a technology-related title designated by the commission pursuant to this act; or (2) is determined through the review process established by this act to perform substantial technical duties.

“Substantial technical duties” means assigned responsibilities involving the design, development, configuration, administration, integration, maintenance, evaluation, or security of technology systems, including software, networks, databases, geographic information systems, artificial intelligence systems, and electronic systems. Routine use of generally available office software, entry of information into an existing system, or use of a technology tool without responsibility for its technical operation shall not, by itself, constitute substantial technical duties.

“Technology-driven workforce reduction” means a proposed elimination of positions, involuntary reduction in scheduled work hours, or displacement of employees for which the implementation of technology is a material contributing cause.

“Approved training provider” means an organization approved under standards established pursuant to section 6 of this act.

4. Identification of technology work

  1. Within 180 days following the effective date of this act, the commission, in consultation with the Office of Information Technology and affected employee representatives, shall establish: (1) an initial list of technology-related titles covered by this act; (2) standards for identifying substantial technical duties; (3) a process through which an agency or employee may request review of assigned technical duties; and (4) procedures for maintaining and updating the list of covered titles.
  2. Each covered agency shall identify employees holding designated titles and positions in which substantial technical duties are regularly assigned.
  3. In evaluating positions outside designated titles, the commission shall consider the nature, complexity, responsibility, and regularity of the assigned duties. [Policy decision: whether to require an additional threshold of ___ percent of assigned work time, measured over ___ months.]
  4. An employee shall not be required to establish eligibility solely through electronic activity monitoring or automated productivity scoring.

5. Classification review and employee protections

  1. When a position is identified as involving substantial technical duties, the commission shall review whether its classification accurately reflects the duties, qualifications, authority, and responsibilities of the position.
  2. The commission shall issue a written determination within [___ days] after receipt of a complete review request.
  3. Where a classification does not accurately reflect assigned duties, the commission shall direct appropriate corrective action through applicable classification procedures.
  4. Identification under this act shall not automatically confer a particular title, salary range, promotion, or bargaining-unit placement.
  5. Nothing in this act shall authorize an agency to reduce an employee’s compensation or accrued seniority solely because the employee requests a review or is identified as a covered employee.
  6. Employees shall receive notice of a determination and access to applicable review or appeal procedures.

6. Annual continuing education

  1. Each covered agency shall provide every covered employee with annual continuing education that includes: (1) cybersecurity instruction relevant to the employee’s responsibilities; and (2) technical instruction relevant to the employee’s assigned duties or anticipated changes in those duties.
  2. The annual requirement shall be [___ hours], including at least [___ hours] of cybersecurity instruction.
  3. Required instruction shall be provided during compensated work time. Required tuition, materials, and assessment fees shall be paid by the employing agency or through funds available under this act.
  4. The commission, in consultation with the Office of Information Technology, shall establish standards for course approval, provider qualifications, learning outcomes, accessibility, and recognition of equivalent training.
  5. Eligible providers may include public institutions of higher education, county colleges, labor-management training programs, labor organizations, nonprofit organizations, and other qualified entities meeting the established standards.
  6. Approval shall be based on published standards. No organization shall receive exclusive provider status under this act.
  7. Completion of an external professional certification examination shall not be required unless expressly designated as necessary for the employee’s duties through otherwise applicable procedures.
  8. An agency shall provide a reasonable make-up opportunity when required instruction is not completed.
  9. Failure to complete instruction required solely by this act shall be reported but shall not, by itself, result in dismissal, demotion, or removal from a technology title.
  10. Agency reports shall distinguish employee noncompletion from failure by the agency to provide accessible instruction, funding, or scheduled participation time.

7. Technology workforce-impact assessment

  1. Before implementing a technology-driven workforce reduction affecting [___ employees or positions within a rolling ___-month period], a covered agency shall prepare a workforce-impact assessment and transition plan.
  2. Related actions arising from the same technology implementation shall be considered together when determining whether the reporting threshold is met.
  3. The assessment shall identify: (1) the technology or operational change involved; (2) the affected titles, positions, and anticipated implementation dates; (3) the expected effects on staffing and employee duties; (4) the basis for the proposed reduction; (5) available training, reassignment, and redeployment options; (6) expected implementation costs and savings; and (7) measures to preserve service continuity, security, and institutional knowledge.
  4. The agency shall submit the assessment to the commission, the Office of Information Technology, and affected employee representatives at least [___ days] before implementation.
  5. Affected employees and their representatives shall have a reasonable opportunity to submit comments and propose alternatives. The agency shall provide a written response before implementation.
  6. A covered agency shall not implement a reduction subject to this section before completing the required assessment, notice, and response process.
  7. Public reporting under this section shall exclude personally identifying information, protected labor-relations information, and information whose disclosure would create a security risk.

8. Transition planning

  1. Before implementing a reduction subject to section 7, the covered agency shall document its consideration of: (1) training for continued service in a modified position; (2) reassignment to a suitable vacant position; (3) redeployment within the agency or another covered agency; (4) changes in implementation timing that would permit an orderly transition; and (5) other feasible alternatives proposed by affected employees or their representatives.
  2. Nothing in this section shall require creation of an unnecessary position or guarantee appointment to a position for which an employee does not satisfy applicable requirements.
  3. This act shall not diminish rights under civil service law, collective negotiations agreements, or other applicable law.

9. Administration and annual reporting

  1. The commission shall administer this act in consultation with the Office of Information Technology.
  2. The commission shall consult affected employee representatives, agency management, and qualified education and training providers when developing implementation standards.
  3. Beginning one year after implementation, the commission shall submit an annual report to the Governor and Legislature describing: (1) covered employees and titles; (2) classification reviews and their disposition; (3) training participation and completion; (4) agency compliance with training-access requirements; (5) program expenditures; (6) workforce-impact assessments and transition outcomes; and (7) recommended statutory, regulatory, or funding changes.
  4. Reports shall use aggregate information and protect confidential employee and security information.

10. Appropriation

There is appropriated from the General Fund to the Civil Service Commission the sum of $1,000,000 for initial implementation of this act, including curriculum development, training delivery, program administration, classification-review support, and evaluation. The commission shall identify anticipated recurring costs and recommend funding for subsequent fiscal years in its annual report.

11. Rules and regulations

The commission, in consultation with the Office of Information Technology, shall adopt rules and regulations necessary to implement this act pursuant to the Administrative Procedure Act.

12. Effective date

This act shall take effect on the 180th day following enactment, except that the commission may take anticipatory administrative action necessary for implementation.

Statement

This bill establishes a State technology workforce development and transition program organized around four duties: identify technology work, train employees, maintain appropriate classifications, and report planned technology-driven workforce reductions.

The bill requires the Civil Service Commission to designate covered technology titles and establish a review process for employees performing substantial technical duties outside those titles. Routine use of software does not, by itself, qualify a position for review as technical work.

Covered employees would receive annual, employer-funded cybersecurity and role-specific technical instruction during compensated work time. The bill requires published standards for training providers and protects employees from dismissal or demotion solely for failure to complete instruction required by this act.

Before implementing specified technology-driven workforce reductions, agencies would prepare an impact assessment, notify affected employee representatives, respond to proposed alternatives, and document consideration of training, reassignment, and redeployment.

The bill appropriates $1 million for initial implementation and requires annual reporting on participation, expenditures, classification reviews, workforce transitions, and future funding needs.
